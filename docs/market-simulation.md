# Market Simulation

## Purpose

This document defines the business-level rules for simulated market price generation in the wealth-platform project.

The simulation is intended to provide deterministic, testable market data for the Brokerage and Portfolio domains without depending on an external market-price API.

This document defines:

- the pricing model;
- the meaning of simulation parameters;
- the Business Day time step;
- the relationship between generated values and persisted Market Prices;
- deterministic testing requirements;
- catch-up behavior when price generation is missed;
- the effect of Security status and parameter changes.

Implementation details such as Java interfaces, random-number libraries, scheduler configuration, database types, transaction annotations, and persistence mappings are deferred to later design documents.

---

## 1. Pricing Model

### MS-001 — Geometric Brownian Motion

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

The initial pricing model is Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM).

For Security price `S_t`, the next simulated price is:

\[
S_{t+\Delta t}
=
S_t
\exp
\left[
\left(
\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2
\right)\Delta t
+
\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} Z
\right]
\]

where:

- `S_t` = previous price;
- `μ` = Expected Return;
- `σ` = Volatility;
- `Δt` = elapsed time expressed in years;
- `Z ~ N(0,1)` = one standard normal random value.

The generated value is an internal simulation value and is not automatically identical to the persisted official Market Price.

---

### MS-002 — Expected Return Is Annualized

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

`Expected Return μ` is an annualized expected return.

Examples:

```text
μ = 0.05   → +5% annual expected return
μ = 0.00   →  0% annual expected return
μ = -0.03  → -3% annual expected return
```

Negative Expected Return is allowed.

---

### MS-003 — Volatility Is Annualized

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

`Volatility σ` is annualized.

The following constraint applies:

```text
σ >= 0
```

`σ = 0` is valid.

When `σ = 0`, the random term disappears and the path becomes deterministic with respect to the remaining inputs.

---

## 2. Time Model

### MS-004 — One Business Day Is One Simulation Step

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

The initial simulation uses one price-generation step per Business Day.

The annualized time step is:

\[
\Delta t = \frac{1}{252}
\]

The initial model assumes 252 trading days per year.

---

### MS-005 — Initial Business Day Calendar

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

The initial Business Day calendar is:

```text
Monday    Business Day
Tuesday   Business Day
Wednesday Business Day
Thursday  Business Day
Friday    Business Day

Saturday  Non-Business Day
Sunday    Non-Business Day
```

Public holidays and exchange-specific calendars are outside the initial scope.

---

### MS-006 — Non-Business Days Do Not Advance the Simulation

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Saturday and Sunday do not consume GBM time steps.

If the scheduler runs on a Non-Business Day, no Market Price is generated.

This is treated as "no generation target" rather than a business failure.

---

## 3. Price Path

### MS-007 — Initial Price Is the Simulation Starting Point

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

`Security.initialPrice` is the starting value of the simulated price path.

The Initial Price itself is not automatically persisted as a Market Price history record.

The first generated Closing Price is produced by applying one GBM step to Initial Price.

```text
Initial Price
    ↓ GBM
First Closing Price
    ↓ persist
Market Price History
```

---

### MS-008 — Later Prices Use the Previous Persisted Closing Price

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

After the first Market Price has been generated, each later Business Day uses the previous persisted Closing Price as `S_t`.

Example:

```text
Initial Price
    ↓
Day 1 Closing Price
    ↓
Day 2 Closing Price
    ↓
Day 3 Closing Price
```

Each Business Day forms one continuous price path.

The simulation must not restart from Initial Price for every Business Day.

---

### MS-009 — Price Generation Is History-Dependent

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

The result for Business Day `t + 1` depends on the persisted Closing Price for the previous simulated Business Day.

Therefore generation order must preserve chronology.

A later Business Day must not be generated by skipping a missing earlier Business Day.

---

## 4. Randomness

### MS-010 — One Standard Normal Value per Security and Business Day

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

For each Security and Business Day, the GBM step consumes one standard normal random value:

```text
Z ~ N(0, 1)
```

The initial model treats Security price movements independently.

Correlation between Securities is outside the initial scope.

---

### MS-011 — Randomness Must Be Controllable in Tests

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

The random input used by the simulation must be replaceable or controllable during tests.

Tests must be able to evaluate cases such as:

```text
Z = 0
Z = 1
Z = -1
```

without depending on uncontrolled random behavior.

The exact Java abstraction or dependency-injection mechanism is deferred to `architecture.md`.

---

### MS-012 — Same Inputs Produce the Same Result

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

For deterministic testing, the same values of:

```text
Previous Price
μ
σ
Δt
Z
```

must produce the same generated price.

This requirement applies to the simulation calculation itself.

---

### MS-013 — Parameter Changes Do Not Reset the Random Process

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Changing Expected Return or Volatility does not reset the price path to Initial Price.

The next generated Market Price continues from the latest persisted Closing Price using the new applicable parameters.

---

## 5. Market Price Normalization

### MS-014 — Simulation Value May Be Fractional

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

The internal GBM calculation may produce a fractional real-number value.

Example:

```text
Generated Value = 1003.728419...
```

The internal value may retain precision until normalization.

---

### MS-015 — Official Closing Price Uses Whole JPY

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Before persistence as an official Market Price, the generated value is rounded to the nearest whole JPY.

Example:

```text
Generated Value
1003.728419...

↓ normalize

Closing Price
1004 JPY
```

The persisted Closing Price is the official price used by Trading and ordinary Portfolio valuation.

---

### MS-016 — Persisted Closing Price Has a Minimum of 1 JPY

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

The persisted Closing Price must be at least:

```text
1 JPY
```

Therefore:

```text
Official Closing Price
= max(1 JPY, rounded generated value)
```

This prevents a positive but very small GBM value from normalizing to `0 JPY`.

---

### MS-017 — Trading Never Uses the Raw Simulation Value

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 3

Buy and Sell Orders use only the persisted official Market Price.

The unnormalized GBM value must not be used as the Execution Price.

---

## 6. Market Price Persistence

### MS-018 — At Most One Market Price per Security and Business Day

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

For each pair:

```text
Security
Business Day
```

there may be at most one persisted Market Price.

---

### MS-019 — Daily Price Generation Is Idempotent

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

If generation is invoked for a Security and Business Day that already has a Market Price:

```text
Create second Market Price  ×
Overwrite existing price    ×
Regenerate different value  ×
```

The existing Market Price remains unchanged.

The concrete uniqueness constraint or persistence mechanism is deferred to `data-model.md` and `sql-strategy.md`.

---

### MS-020 — Historical Market Prices Are Immutable

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Persisted historical Market Prices are not recalculated, overwritten, or deleted through normal simulation operations.

Historical prices represent already-generated market facts.

---

### MS-021 — Business Date and Processing Time Are Different Concepts

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

A Market Price is associated with the Business Day for which it represents the Closing Price.

The Business Date is distinct from the actual wall-clock time at which the scheduler or recovery process generated the record.

The concrete persistence fields are deferred to `data-model.md`.

---

## 7. Scheduler and Catch-Up Behavior

### MS-022 — Missing Business Days Are Generated by Catch-Up

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

If Market Price generation is missed for one or more Business Days, later recovery must generate the missing prices before generating later dependent prices.

Example:

```text
Friday Closing Price exists

Monday generation missed
Tuesday scheduler runs

Required order:

Friday Close
    ↓
Monday Close
    ↓
Tuesday Close
```

Tuesday must not be generated directly from Friday while Monday is missing.

---

### MS-023 — Catch-Up Runs in Chronological Order

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Missing Market Prices are generated from the oldest missing Business Day to the newest.

Chronological ordering is required because each GBM step depends on the previous Closing Price.

---

### MS-024 — New Securities Do Not Receive Pre-Registration History

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

A newly registered Security becomes eligible for simulation from its registration date onward.

The simulation does not backfill artificial Market Prices for Business Days before the Security was registered in the system.

---

### MS-025 — Failure Is Isolated per Security and Business Day

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Generation of one Security for one Business Day must either succeed completely or leave that Security/Business-Day price ungenerated.

A partially persisted Market Price is prohibited.

---

### MS-026 — Failure of One Security Does Not Require Global Rollback

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

If one Security fails to generate while another Security succeeds:

```text
Security ABC → ungenerated
Security XYZ → generated
```

is allowed.

Successful generation for unrelated Securities does not need to be rolled back.

The failed Security can be recovered later through Catch-Up.

---

## 8. Security Status and Simulation

### MS-027 — Disabled Securities Continue Price Generation

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Both of the following statuses remain simulation targets:

```text
ACTIVE    → generate Market Price
DISABLED  → generate Market Price
```

`DISABLED` means new acquisition is prohibited.

It does not mean that market data generation stops.

This rule allows existing holders to continue receiving current prices and, under Brokerage rules, to sell an existing Position.

---

## 9. Security Parameter Changes

### MS-028 — Parameter Changes Do Not Rewrite History

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

Changing:

- Expected Return `μ`;
- Volatility `σ`;

does not modify any already-persisted Market Price.

Parameter changes affect only future generation.

---

### MS-029 — Parameter Changes Apply to the Next Not-Yet-Generated Market Price

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

A parameter change takes effect for the next Market Price that has not yet been generated.

Example 1:

```text
Monday price not yet generated
Administrator changes σ

Monday generation
→ use new σ
```

Example 2:

```text
Monday price already generated
Administrator changes σ

Monday historical price
→ unchanged

Tuesday generation
→ use new σ
```

This resolves the prior effective-timing Open Question.

---

### MS-030 — Parameter Changes Continue from the Existing Price Path

**Status:** Confirmed  
**Phase:** Phase 2

When parameters change, the next GBM step uses:

```text
latest persisted Closing Price
+
currently applicable μ
+
currently applicable σ
```

The simulation does not restart from Initial Price.

---

## 10. Interaction with Other Domains

### Trading

Trading requires:

- the current date to be a Business Day;
- a persisted Market Price for that same Business Day.

Trading never triggers price generation itself.

---

### Portfolio

Portfolio valuation uses the latest persisted Market Price.

Unlike Trading, Portfolio valuation may use the most recent previous Business Day's Closing Price when no price exists for the current calendar date.

---

### Administration

Administrators may update simulation parameters and enable or disable Securities.

Such changes never rewrite historical Market Prices.

---

## 11. Testing Expectations

The simulation should support deterministic TDD cases including:

### Deterministic Random Input

```text
Given Z = 0
When one GBM step is calculated
Then the result is reproducible
```

### Positive Random Shock

```text
Given Z = 1
When one GBM step is calculated
Then the expected formula result is produced
```

### Negative Random Shock

```text
Given Z = -1
When one GBM step is calculated
Then the expected formula result is produced
```

### Zero Volatility

```text
Given σ = 0
When a price is generated
Then the random term contributes nothing
```

### Duplicate Generation

```text
Given a Market Price already exists
When generation runs again for the same Security and Business Day
Then no second Market Price is created
And the original price is unchanged
```

### Catch-Up

```text
Given Monday price is missing
And Friday price exists
When generation runs on Tuesday
Then Monday is generated before Tuesday
```

### Parameter Change

```text
Given Monday price already exists
When σ is changed
Then Monday remains unchanged
And Tuesday uses the new σ
```

These are behavioral expectations only. Concrete test classes and infrastructure belong in `test-strategy.md`.

## 12. Planned Future Scope / Future Considerations

The following are not part of the initial GBM simulation:

### Calendar

- public-holiday calendars;
- exchange-specific calendars.

### Market Data

- Open / High / Low / Close (OHLC);
- intraday prices;
- trading volume.

### Price Dynamics

- Security correlation;
- market-wide shocks;
- mean reversion;
- stochastic volatility;
- jump diffusion;
- circuit breakers.

### Corporate Events

- stock splits;
- dividends;
- other corporate actions.

### External Integration

- real market-price APIs.

These items remain future candidates unless separately promoted to Planned Future Scope.
